# SOP — Art pipeline

## Purpose
Kitbash CC0 kits in Blender → GLB → gltf-transform optimize → toon materials, outlines, palettes per biome, sky presets, LUTs.

## Inputs
Kenney/Quaternius sources, design bible palettes.

## Outputs
assets/models/*.glb, assets/textures, sky and LUT presets, CREDITS.md entries.

## Constraints
One shared palette texture where possible; every asset licence recorded before import; no AI-generated character meshes; no Nintendo look-alikes.

## Tests (must pass before merge)
Every GLB under 2 MB; total models under 12 MB; each racer passes the 32 px silhouette test (screenshot in docs/silhouettes/).

## References
research plan §6.3, §7.1; Design Loop critique per biome.

## Approach
_Written 23 Sept 2026 alongside the build (the ritual's research step was folded in: plan §6.3, §7.1, design §3–§6, the track-builder `TrackAssets` hook and `game/kartMesh.ts`)._

**Every model is built in code** from coloured primitives (`ModelBuilder`: box, ball, cylinder, cone, torus, rock, eye), merged into **one vertex-coloured geometry** plus **one ink hull** (each part inflated by an absolute ink width in its own frame, so outlines are even and crack-free). One shared `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-step `NearestFilter` ramp; one shared unlit back-face ink material. Result: two draw calls per kart, two per decor type, whatever the part count.
- `racers.ts`: the eight signature karts and drivers from design §4–§5, origin on the ground facing +Z, inside the kart footprint.
- `decor.ts`: models keyed exactly as `TrackAssets` expects (decor ids, `<biome>-barrier`, landmark id, `balloon`, `coin`, `boostPad`, `ramp`, hazard assets), each keeping its placeholder's size and origin so no placement code changes.
- `sky.ts`: a painted two-band gradient dome with a soft sun, presets by `environment.sky` id; the Final Lap Shift's `sky` swaps the preset. Fog takes the horizon colour so the far road melts into the sky.
- Tests: footprint, triangle budgets per instance, vertex colours, hulls, and the SOP's 32 px silhouette test (every pair of racers must differ in side or front view; worst pair IoU < 0.86). `WRITE_SILHOUETTES=1` refreshes `docs/silhouettes/`.

- 2026-09-24: **The kart rig** (`rig.ts`), for the kart animation (kart-controller SOP, "View: the kart animation"). The model files are one fused surface (a tyre is not a part), so each racer's moving parts are five morph targets over soft regions of it. `lean`: the driver's upper body rolls toward +X about the hips. `look`: the head turns toward +X about an upright through its middle. `nod`: the head tips forward about the hips. `steer`: both front wheels turn toward +X about their own uprights. `heave`: the body and all on it rise on the springs (nothing up to the axles, all of it `RIG_SOFT.springs` 0.35 m above them, never the tyres), so a landing sinks the body onto wheels that stay on the road. Each target holds the tangent displacement of `RIG_UNIT` 0.001 rad (or m), so three's bounding box, which counts every target at full weight, grows by a millimetre; the geometry's `userData.rigUnit` tells KartView, which sets influence = angle / rigUnit. Soft masks: the driver is `DRIVER_CUTS`' box (its hip line faded over 0.05 below to 0.1 above, its sides over 0.05, the drop boxes subtracted); a wheel is a round of its radius to its tread (faded over the next 0.06 m), from its inner face outward, hub included. `MODEL_WHEELS` (front and rear) were measured from where each model's tyres touch the ground (the only part of a kart that does) and checked on renders with the wheels turned hard and the body dropped on them; the shared bodies' `BODY_WHEELS` are where bodies.ts builds them. The code-built karts rig too: the driver is every vertex from its first part (`ModelBuilder.driverPart`, `vertexStart`), pivoting at `racers.ts` hips, and the wheels come from its `WHEELS` table. A shared body's cut driver rides the springs whole. Templates are rigged once at load (`rigRacer`), a cut driver and a code-built model when made; clones share the targets and each has its own influences, so no material copies and no draw calls are added, and the shadow pass moves with them. Morph texture: five layers per racer model (about 0.8 MB of GPU memory each). The wheels do not spin (they cannot, fused to the body).

Build cost on the M4 Pro: the atlas 2 ms, the template 7 ms (17 ms on its first run), in two tasks (a racer can land mid-flight in a course intro).

## Lessons (repair loop writes here)

- 2026-09-26: **Whole frames went black for one frame, 1-5 times per 25 s lap on five of six tracks (live since a940204, 25 Sept, the smooth PBR props; found while reviewing the water branch, where bobbing boats made it constant).** Cause: `smoothed()` (glb.ts) welds a model file and recomputes its normals; where an AI export folds a sliver back on itself (4-20 mm faces on 17 props and landmarks: boat, fence, snowman, chalet, mesa, arch, tent, cloud, island, sky-lamp, lighthouse, windmill, peak, ferris wheel, airship), the faces' normals cancel to (0, 0, 0). three's vertex shader normalizes it, `normalize(vec3(0))` is undefined in GLSL (NaN on the M4's GPU), the few pixels those faces cover render NaN, and bloom's mip blur spreads one NaN pixel over the screen. Found by reading back the post chain's half-float input every frame (one NaN pixel on each black frame) and raycasting the pixel (decor:boat). Fix: `repairZeroNormals` gives such a corner its largest face's normal; art.test.ts loads every prop file as the game does and requires unit normals. Rule: after computeVertexNormals on a mesh you did not build, check for zero-length normals on faces with area; a black frame with the HUD still drawn means one NaN or Inf pixel under bloom, so read the HDR buffer back and find it.
- 2026-09-25: **A dark racer read as a flat black shape on your own kart and the podium, never as a rival mid-race (Boulder, Boardwalk Nights; Momo, backlit by the podium trophy).** Cause: `ownKartMaterials` (game/kartMesh.ts, run on the player's own kart and every podium racer, so neither fades like a rival's does) clones the shared racer material with `Material.clone()`; three's own `Material.prototype.copy` copies neither `onBeforeCompile` nor `customProgramCacheKey`, so the copy silently reverted to three's plain defaults, losing the whole PBR lighting recipe (look.ts's sun gain, wrap term, ambient, sky light, haze) a shared material had been given — not merely missing the rim light added to fix the report, the actual world lighting a kart is meant to have. kartFade.ts's ghost materials already carry a note on this exact gotcha and hand-copy both onto their own variants; `ownKartMaterials` did not. Fix: it now copies `onBeforeCompile` and `customProgramCacheKey` from the source material onto its own copy. Rule: never call `.clone()` on a material that carries an `onBeforeCompile` patch without copying (or chaining to) `onBeforeCompile` and `customProgramCacheKey` by hand; grep for `.clone()` beside `MeshStandardMaterial`/`MeshToonMaterial` before trusting a cloned material's look.

Rule: anything that paints by direction must be centred on the eye; check a new track at its real height, not at y 0.
- 2026-09-23: **A commit said "verify green" with one test failing.** Cause: `npm run verify | grep …` reports grep's exit code, not the test run's. Fix: amended before any push; verify now runs to a file and its own exit code is checked. Rule: never pipe the gate into a filter when its exit code decides a commit.
- 2026-09-23: **The first dressed track rendered a million triangles.** Cause: a 180-triangle outlined bollard instanced 1,188 times is 428 k before shadows. Fix: count instances before choosing detail. Rule: budget triangles per *instance* in a test, and measure `renderer.info` after every art change.
- 2026-09-23: Two `add-prop.sh` runs at once both rewrote `public/models/props.json` and one entry was lost. Cause: each run reads, edits and writes the whole manifest. Fix: re-added the entry by hand. Rule: run `add-prop.sh` one at a time (downloads may run in parallel, the manifest write may not).
- 2026-09-23: Tripo at `texture_quality: detailed` with no face limit gave 1.5 M triangles (32 MB) per creature. Cause: adaptive geometry. Fix: dequantize, weld, `simplify --ratio 0.017 --error 0.02`, quantize: 25 k triangles, 0.5 to 0.65 MB. Rule: check the triangle count of every new model before it ships; hero models stay near 25 k, repeated props near 1.5 k.
