# SOP — Art pipeline

## Purpose
Kitbash CC0 kits in Blender → GLB → gltf-transform optimize → toon materials, outlines, palettes per biome, sky presets, LUTs.

## Inputs
Kenney/Quaternius sources, design bible palettes.

## Outputs
assets/models/*.glb, assets/textures, sky and LUT presets, CREDITS.md entries.

## Constraints
One shared palette texture where possible; every asset licence recorded before import; no AI-generated character meshes; no Nintendo look-alikes.

## Tests (must pass before merge)
Every GLB under 2 MB; total models under 12 MB; each racer passes the 32 px silhouette test (screenshot in docs/silhouettes/).

## References
research plan §6.3, §7.1; Design Loop critique per biome.

## Approach
_Written 23 Sept 2026 alongside the build (the ritual's research step was folded in: plan §6.3, §7.1, design §3–§6, the track-builder `TrackAssets` hook and `game/kartMesh.ts`)._

**Every model is built in code** from coloured primitives (`ModelBuilder`: box, ball, cylinder, cone, torus, rock, eye), merged into **one vertex-coloured geometry** plus **one ink hull** (each part inflated by an absolute ink width in its own frame, so outlines are even and crack-free). One shared `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-step `NearestFilter` ramp; one shared unlit back-face ink material. Result: two draw calls per kart, two per decor type, whatever the part count.
- `racers.ts`: the eight signature karts and drivers from design §4–§5, origin on the ground facing +Z, inside the kart footprint.
- `decor.ts`: models keyed exactly as `TrackAssets` expects (decor ids, `<biome>-barrier`, landmark id, `balloon`, `coin`, `boostPad`, `ramp`, hazard assets), each keeping its placeholder's size and origin so no placement code changes.
- `sky.ts`: a painted two-band gradient dome with a soft sun, presets by `environment.sky` id; the Final Lap Shift's `sky` swaps the preset. Fog takes the horizon colour so the far road melts into the sky.
- Tests: footprint, triangle budgets per instance, vertex colours, hulls, and the SOP's 32 px silhouette test (every pair of racers must differ in side or front view; worst pair IoU < 0.86). `WRITE_SILHOUETTES=1` refreshes `docs/silhouettes/`.

- 2026-09-24: **The kart rig** (`rig.ts`), for the kart animation (kart-controller SOP, "View: the kart animation"). The model files are one fused surface (a tyre is not a part), so each racer's moving parts are five morph targets over soft regions of it. `lean`: the driver's upper body rolls toward +X about the hips. `look`: the head turns toward +X about an upright through its middle. `nod`: the head tips forward about the hips. `steer`: both front wheels turn toward +X about their own uprights. `heave`: the body and all on it rise on the springs (nothing up to the axles, all of it `RIG_SOFT.springs` 0.35 m above them, never the tyres), so a landing sinks the body onto wheels that stay on the road. Each target holds the tangent displacement of `RIG_UNIT` 0.001 rad (or m), so three's bounding box, which counts every target at full weight, grows by a millimetre; the geometry's `userData.rigUnit` tells KartView, which sets influence = angle / rigUnit. Soft masks: the driver is `DRIVER_CUTS`' box (its hip line faded over 0.05 below to 0.1 above, its sides over 0.05, the drop boxes subtracted); a wheel is a round of its radius to its tread (faded over the next 0.06 m), from its inner face outward, hub included. `MODEL_WHEELS` (front and rear) were measured from where each model's tyres touch the ground (the only part of a kart that does) and checked on renders with the wheels turned hard and the body dropped on them; the shared bodies' `BODY_WHEELS` are where bodies.ts builds them. The code-built karts rig too: the driver is every vertex from its first part (`ModelBuilder.driverPart`, `vertexStart`), pivoting at `racers.ts` hips, and the wheels come from its `WHEELS` table. A shared body's cut driver rides the springs whole. Templates are rigged once at load (`rigRacer`), a cut driver and a code-built model when made; clones share the targets and each has its own influences, so no material copies and no draw calls are added, and the shadow pass moves with them. Morph texture: five layers per racer model (about 0.8 MB of GPU memory each). The wheels do not spin (they cannot, fused to the body).

- 2026-09-27: **The first ranking run died halfway (window.__idPass is not a function).** Cause: editing vista.ts while it ran made Vite reload the dev page, which wiped the hooks the check had installed. - 2026-09-26: **Firefox (2-3 warnings a race, every load) and sometimes Safari logged "texSubImage: Offset+size must be <= the size of the existing specified image", and the ground and road detail maps stayed flat for the session.** Cause: `detailTexture` drew a 4 x 4 flat stand-in until the albedo arrived, then swapped in the 512 x 512 relief on the same texture; WebGL2 fixes a texture's size at its first upload (three's texStorage2D), so whenever the stand-in was drawn first the relief upload failed. Chrome on a fast local load drew the relief first, so it never showed there. Found by a cross-engine sweep of the live site, reproduced with Playwright Firefox and the textures delayed (2 warnings at 0 ms, 3 at 5 s; 0 and 0 after the fix). Fix: the stand-in is DETAIL_SIZE square (detail.dom.test.ts). Rule: never swap a bigger image into a texture that may already be on the GPU: make the stand-in the final size, or give the texture a new Source on a new Texture.
- 2026-09-26: **Whole frames went black for one frame, 1-5 times per 25 s lap on five of six tracks (live since a940204, 25 Sept, the smooth PBR props; found while reviewing the water branch, where bobbing boats made it constant).** Cause: `smoothed()` (glb.ts) welds a model file and recomputes its normals; where an AI export folds a sliver back on itself (4-20 mm faces on 17 props and landmarks: boat, fence, snowman, chalet, mesa, arch, tent, cloud, island, sky-lamp, lighthouse, windmill, peak, ferris wheel, airship), the faces' normals cancel to (0, 0, 0). three's vertex shader normalizes it, `normalize(vec3(0))` is undefined in GLSL (NaN on the M4's GPU), the few pixels those faces cover render NaN, and bloom's mip blur spreads one NaN pixel over the screen. Found by reading back the post chain's half-float input every frame (one NaN pixel on each black frame) and raycasting the pixel (decor:boat). Fix: `repairZeroNormals` gives such a corner its largest face's normal; art.test.ts loads every prop file as the game does and requires unit normals. Rule: after computeVertexNormals on a mesh you did not build, check for zero-length normals on faces with area; a black frame with the HUD still drawn means one NaN or Inf pixel under bloom, so read the HDR buffer back and find it.
- 2026-09-26: **The reviewer's own low, still `kart.photo()` shot beside a boat went black on 2 of 4 frames; re-diagnosed as "the camera under the swell," but the numbers do not support that for this exact shot, and it would not explain what was actually seen.** Checked: Harbour's `track.groundPlaneY` is -1.66 m; the reviewer's own camera Y was 0.9 (an absolute world height, not "0.9 m over the water" as the wording suggested) — 2.56 m clear of the flat sea, nowhere near the tallest crest (about 1.05 m). Re-running the *exact* same camera (position, look, fov, timing) three times gave three different results: the original run's first two of four frames black, one repeat with none black at all, another with the same first-two-black pattern — not a deterministic function of the camera's own position or angle, which "camera under the swell" (or the shallow-angle theory from the previous round) would both have to be. It also happens with no water anywhere in the frame (a shallow-angle shot inland, aimed at the grass past the shoulder, went black on one run and not on a repeat). Fix: none found — CAM.seaClear and seaLevel were still strengthened anyway (finding 1 above), since the underlying safety gap they close is real regardless of what caused these particular black frames, and it is worth having proven safe by a test rather than by this one flaky repro. Rule: before accepting a plausible, numeric-sounding diagnosis for an intermittent bug, re-run the *exact* reported repro (same tool, same numbers) and check it reproduces every time before trusting the mechanism it points to; a diagnosis that "fits" the shape of the bug is not the same as one the numbers actually support once measured. Solved in review round 5 (Decisions): one NaN pixel from a prop's zero-length normal, spread by bloom; it did happen in play too (1-5 black flashes per 25 s lap on five of six tracks), not only after kart.photo() cuts.
- 2026-09-26: **A washed-out grey band ran parallel to the shore, in the shallows, on both sea tracks.** Cause: the alpha curve (`WATER_DEPTH.alphaAt0`/`opaqueAt`) spent a wide depth band still mostly see-through and barely colour-shifted, so the (deliberately pale, nearly neutral-grey) wet-sand vertex colour underneath (land.ts) showed through a barely-blue overlay — a flat, desaturated stripe distinct from both the bright foam nearer the sand and the fully-tinted opaque water further out, easy to mistake for a mesh edge since it tracked a fairly constant depth (hence a constant distance from a straight shore). Fix: a shorter, steeper ramp (`alphaAt0` 0.3 → 0.12, `opaqueAt` 2.5 → 1.7 m, `colorAt` 4.0 → 2.3 m) leaves far less depth range where an under-tinted overlay can wash out on a pale seabed. Rule: a translucency curve that blends toward a *tinted* colour needs to reach a real share of that tint quickly, not just a share of full alpha — checked separately, they can each look fine in isolation and still band once multiplied together over a pale surface.
- 2026-09-26 (review round 3, 5 findings on the round-2 submission): all five addressed. **1 — the black frames the reviewer's own low, still camera hit: re-diagnosed as the camera under the swell, not a dev-tool quirk.** Whatever the real cause of the specific black frames turned out to be (see the Lessons entry below — it was not this), the underlying safety gap was real and is now closed everywhere: `game/camera.ts` `CAM.seaClear` is no longer a flat 1.2 m but `WAVE_MAX_HEIGHT + 0.3` (imported from waterWaves.ts, so it can never quietly fall out of sync with the wave amplitudes again), and `seaLevel(track)` folds in `SEA_TIDE.rise` (below), so every camera's floor is measured against the sea's real, current surface, crest and tide both. A new shared `clampAboveSea(pos, track)` replaced the two hand-written clamps in camera.ts and was added to three cameras that had no sea floor at all before: `game/celebrate.ts` FinishCam (the swing can pass a coastal finish line), `game/intro.ts`'s low road moves (`onRoad`, Harbour's own pier-ramp glide), and main.ts's attract-mode `tvCamera` (the leader it orbits can be down by the coast). camera.test.ts gained the reviewer's own explicit ask, a test that the floor clears the tallest crest, plus one that walks every water track at three tide levels. **2 — the tide (Harbour's own flood, +0.7 m) could combine with a full crest to reach over the road, and the near-camera wave grid did not rise with the far flat plane.** `waterWaves.ts` gained `SEA_TIDE` (`rise`, written every frame by shiftStage.ts's own `seaRise` piece through a new `TrackAssets.tide` hook — the same pattern as Frostbite's `FROST_LAKE`/`LakeHook`, so track-builder still never imports art-pipeline; `scale`, a getter, always `tideScale(rise.value)`, never written separately, so the two can never drift apart) and `tideScale(metres)`: 1 with no tide, shrinking to a 0.4 floor by the time Harbour's own 0.7 m is fully in, tuned against Harbour's own measured numbers (lowest road over the sea 1.53 m; at full tide the crest clears the tide-raised road by about 0.40 m, comfortably over the reviewer's own 0.3 m ask — the exact arithmetic is a test in waterWaves.test.ts, not just a claim here). The wave grid's own `attachWaveFollow` now sets `position.y = waterY + SEA_TIDE.rise.value` every frame (it already ran every frame for the x/z cell-snap); the shader's own amplitude gets an extra `uTideFade` factor (wired straight to `SEA_TIDE.scale`, live, like `WATER_CLOCK`); a floating boat's own `floatRide` scales by the same factor and adds the same rise, so a boat also rides the tide, not just a shrunken swell at the old height. `waterMaterial()` resets `SEA_TIDE.rise` to 0 on every call (every scene build, cache hit or not), so a previous race's tide can never leak into the next one — checked by a test. Not confirmed on a live, played-through flood (getting a real race to the shift needs several minutes of simulated laps; the dev `kart.step()` fast-forward did not reach it in the time this review had): the whole mechanism is unit-tested end to end (the tide value, the derived scale, the wave grid's own y, a boat's own bob, the camera floor, all at 0, half and Harbour's own full 0.7 m) but not eyeballed at the real event. **3 — facets in the sky's own reflection from an overview camera.** The analytic normal (`lkGerstnerNormal`) was a per-vertex value, linearly interpolated across each triangle — fine for the fine, near-camera rings of the graded grid, visibly faceted on the coarse far rings and the flat plane's own two huge triangles, exactly where a specular highlight (the sky's reflection, the sun glint) makes normal error visible in a way position error never is. Fix: the vertex shader keeps its own `lkGerstner` call for displacement and `vCrest` (interpolating a scalar blend weight is fine), but no longer computes or carries `vGerstnerNormal`; the fragment shader now calls `lkGerstnerNormal(vWorld.xz, time, fade)` itself, `fade` recomputed there from `vWorld` (the same formula the vertex shader uses from `rest.xz`, one extra `length` and `smoothstep`, cheaper than a second varying). Confirmed on the reviewer's own overview camera (a fresh shot at the same spot): the reflection reads smooth. **4 — re-shot the reviewer's own `water-look2.mjs` unmodified** against this worktree's build: the boat's own grid slot and position matched exactly (determinism intact); `overview-a`/`overview-b` show the fixed grid and normal cleanly; the low `swell-*` sequence still showed two black frames out of four on the very first run — see the Lessons entry: re-running the identical camera sequence twice more reproduced it inconsistently (sometimes 0 black frames, sometimes 2), which is what sent the investigation toward "flaky render glitch," not "camera under the swell," for this specific case. **5 — Boardwalk Nights got the same overview and shore pass:** its own ferris-wheel overview shows the identical smooth-reflection fix; its own chase camera is unaffected; its pier posts' translucency (round 2's own fix, untouched this round) still shows plainly. - 2026-09-25: **A dark racer read as a flat black shape on your own kart and the podium, never as a rival mid-race (Boulder, Boardwalk Nights; Momo, backlit by the podium trophy).** Cause: `ownKartMaterials` (game/kartMesh.ts, run on the player's own kart and every podium racer, so neither fades like a rival's does) clones the shared racer material with `Material.clone()`; three's own `Material.prototype.copy` copies neither `onBeforeCompile` nor `customProgramCacheKey`, so the copy silently reverted to three's plain defaults, losing the whole PBR lighting recipe (look.ts's sun gain, wrap term, ambient, sky light, haze) a shared material had been given — not merely missing the rim light added to fix the report, the actual world lighting a kart is meant to have. kartFade.ts's ghost materials already carry a note on this exact gotcha and hand-copy both onto their own variants; `ownKartMaterials` did not.
