# SOP — Kart controller

## Purpose
Kinematic arcade kart: speed, heading, lateral slip, hop, drift with 3-tier mini-turbo, boosts with priority, surface grip, ground snap.

## Inputs
InputState per tick; TrackDefinition LUT (height, normal, surface at t); KartArchetype multipliers.

## Outputs
Updated kart entry in RaceState; events: driftTierUp, boostStart, landed, hit.

## Constraints
No rigid-body physics. All constants from docs/schemas/kart defaults. Deterministic at 120 Hz. Non-stacking boosts, Trick > Item > Drift.

## Tests (must pass before merge)
Headless lap on Harbour Loop with a scripted input log finishes within ±2% of the target time; drift tiers fire at the documented seconds; boost never exceeds 1.4× top speed.

## References
turbo-kart-rush Kart.ts; Mario-Kart-3.js drift code; research plan §4.1–4.2.

## Approach
_Synthesised 7 Sept 2026 from turbo-kart-rush `src/kart/Kart.ts`, Mario-Kart-3.js `PlayerController.jsx`, Starter-Kit-Racing `js/Vehicle.js`, research plan §4.1–4.2, §4.5–4.6, §6.4 and appendix A§2, B§2, B§5._

### What we take from the references
- **turbo-kart-rush** is the closest match: scalar kinematic model (forward speed + lateral velocity + vertical velocity), 120 Hz fixed step, hop → drift → 3-tier charge state machine, grip per surface, sticky ground snap, wall push-out, weight-class bumps. We copy its *shape*, not its code. We do not copy: re-deriving heading/slip from world velocity every tick (float drift), flat boost strength for all tiers, the `otherId & 31` cooldown table, its lack of render interpolation, and its 190-line `resolveTrack`.
- **Mario-Kart-3.js** gives two feel tricks: turn rate that scales with speed, and a visible chassis yaw that lags the logical heading. Its drift tiers are cosmetic and its timing uses GSAP tweens; nothing else is portable.
- **Starter-Kit-Racing** rides a rigid-body sphere. Rejected by the constraint above. We keep one idea: body pitch and roll are driven by an acceleration-lag term, not by physics.
- **Nobody** has slipstream. We design it from plan §4.1 and STK's 8×4 zone.

### The model (one kart, one tick)
State is the `karts[i]` entry from `race-state.schema.json`. Nothing else. No Three.js objects in the sim. All velocities are in the kart's own frame.
- `speed` m/s along `heading`; `lateralVelocity` m/s to the right; `verticalVelocity` m/s up.
- `t` is the nearest spline fraction; `position` is world metres.

Per tick, in this order:
1. **Timers** — boost, spin, slow, drift charge multiplier, slipstream, intangible. All count down by `dt`.
2. **Status gate** — if `spinRemaining > 0`: ignore input, decay `speed` to 0 over the spin, skip to step 8.
3. **Target speed** — `V = base.topSpeed × speedClasses[cc] × (1 + archetype.speed) × (1 + coins × coinBonusEach)`, then `× boost.multiplier` if a boost is live, then capped by `status.slowedTo` if set. Then the **surface cap**: `V = min(V, base.topSpeed × ... × surfaceSpeed[surface])`, skipped entirely while a boost is live (`boostIgnoresSurfaceCap`) or while airborne (`airborneIgnoresSurfaceCap`). `speed` moves toward `V` at `accel × (1 + archetype.accel) × (accelLaunch − accelTaper × (v / V)²)` when throttling (strong off the line, tapering at the top), at `brake` when braking, and coasts at `coastDecel` with no input. Above `V` it decays at `overSpeedDecel` (or at `brake` if braking harder) so a boost tails off instead of stopping dead, and so hitting mud bleeds speed over about half a second instead of slamming. Brake at rest → reverse at `reverseFraction × V`.
4. **Steer** — normal: `yaw = steer × steerRate × (1 + archetype.handling) × f(speed)`, with `f(v) = min(1, v / (steerLowSpeed × V)) × (1 − steerFalloff × v / V)`. This gives a 30 m turn radius at top speed (STK) and a tight pivot at low speed. Reverse flips the sign. Drifting: `yaw = drift.direction × steerRate × lerp(driftSteerMin, driftSteerMax, drift.yawK) × driftSpeedScale`, where `yawK` chases the whole stick range `(1 + steer × direction) / 2` over `driftYawLag` (full out 0 = the wide line, centred 0.5 = the medium line, full in 1 = the tight line) and `driftSpeedScale` tightens it below top speed as the grip turn tightens, so a full-inward drift is never wider than steering at the same speed. Outward drift only.
5. **Rotate the velocity into the new frame** — when heading turns by `Δθ`: `speed' = speed cosΔθ + lat sinΔθ`, `lat' = lat cosΔθ − speed sinΔθ`. Then damp: `lat -= lat × grip × dt`, with `grip = base.grip<surface> × trackQuery.gripScale` (× `airGrip` in the air). This is the whole slide model: ice slides because grip is low, nothing else.
6. **Hop / drift state machine** — `idle → hopping → drifting → idle`. Press drift while grounded and `speed ≥ driftMinSpeed × V` → hop (`verticalVelocity = hopVelocity`, plus ground climb rate on a slope). Land within `hopSeconds` with stick held → drift locks `direction = sign(steer)`. Grounded and idle with the button still held, fast enough and `|steer| ≥ driftLateSteer` → the drift locks at once, no hop (a late drift, or one straight off a landing). Charge each tick: `+chargeFull` with the stick centred or into the drift, `+chargeNeutral` pushed out of it, scaled by `dt × 60` because the schema numbers are per 60 fps frame, and multiplied by `drift.chargeMultiplier` while the Rocket Lolly timer runs. Tier = count of `driftTiers` passed; emit `driftTierUp` on each change. Release → boost from tier (`boostMultiplier`, `boostSeconds[tier − 1]`), tier 0 → nothing. Cancel with no boost if `speed < driftKeepSpeed × V`, airborne longer than `driftAirCancelSeconds`, or hit. An optional `maxDriftTier` on the step options caps the tier (Smart Steer later).
7. **Boost arbitration** — one live boost. Priorities: trick 5 > item 4 > pad 3 > drift 2 > slipstream 1 = start 1. A new boost runs now if nothing is live, or if its priority is higher and it is at least as strong (what is left of the live one goes underneath); the same source refreshes to the longer time. Otherwise (lower priority, weaker, or an equal-priority shorter one) it goes underneath. The one underneath (`boostQueue`, one slot, the stronger then longer kept) keeps its own clock and takes over for what it has left when the live one ends. Emit `boostStart` when a boost starts running on request. Boosts never add.
8. **Gravity and ground** — `verticalVelocity -= gravity × dt`. Query the track at the kart's `t` and lateral offset for `groundY`, `normal`, `surface`, `halfWidth`. If `y ≤ groundY + groundStick` and `verticalVelocity ≤ groundLaunchVy`: snap, `grounded = true`, `verticalVelocity = 0`, emit `landed` if it was airborne (and fire the queued trick boost). Otherwise airborne. Leaving a `jumps[]` ramp sets `verticalVelocity = max(verticalVelocity, launch)` and `airborne.fromJumpId`, for a grounded kart or one mid-hop at a ramp's lip (not a trick bump's crest); the drift button while airborne from a jump sets `trickQueued`, and so does a press in the last `trickBufferSeconds` before a ramp's launch (both emit `trick`). The ground step also caches the ground normal, ramps and bumps included, on `groundNormal` for the view. Below `voidY` → emit `respawn` (race-manager does the reset).
9. **Integrate** — `position += (forward × speed + right × lat) × dt`; `y += verticalVelocity × dt`. Update `t` by a local search ±`tSearchWindow` around the previous `t` (never a global search: it is slow and it jumps at crossings).
10. **Walls** — if `|lateral offset| > halfWidth − kartRadius`: push back to the edge, reflect the outward part of the world velocity by `wallRestitution` and keep the rest (never re-pointed along the nose). Once per impact (`wallCooldown`), a hard hit (outward / total > `hardWallFraction`) scrubs from nothing at that line up to `wallScrub` head-on, and turns the nose `wallDeflect` of the way to the new direction of travel (the view eases the snap); while pressed on the wall after that, the nose swings toward the wall line at up to `wallDeflectRate`. Emit a `hit`-free `wall` event with the cooldown.
11. **Kart vs kart** — circles of `kartRadius`. Only karts within `contactHeight` of each other in height touch (the items' rule). On overlap: the closing speed along the contact normal is cancelled every tick, split by mass (karts are solid: a rear-ender pushes, never passes through), the overlap eases apart at `max(bumpSeparateRate, closing speed)` in proportion to inverse mass, and once per `bumpCooldownSeconds` the lighter kart gets `bumpForce × massRatio` lateral. `mass = 1 + archetype.weight + (boost live ? dashMassBonus : 0) + (status.shield ? shieldMass : 0)`. This is the heavy class's whole mechanical hook, and it is the one weight rule every Mario Kart has: heavies push further and resist being pushed. The dash-mass term is MK8DX's, and it is what stops "heavy always wins" being boring — a boosting light kart wins the bump. Ghosts and intangible karts skip this.
12. **Slipstream** — in another kart's wake (behind it within `slipstreamLength`, within `slipstreamHalfWidth` and `contactHeight`, moving the same way) accumulate `slipstreamSeconds`; at `base.slipstreamSeconds` grant the slipstream boost and reset.
13. **Hit** — `applyHit(kind)`: drops `hitCoinsLost` coins. Then `spinRemaining = hitSpinSeconds`, coins or not, as in Mario Kart 8 Deluxe and World (Adam, 24 Sept 2026; `coinShield`, the old buffer that swapped the spin for a slow while coins were held, is a schema switch, off). Either way the drift and the live boost are cancelled. Emit `hit`.

Render side (`KartView`, view.ts): holds previous and current sim pose, lerps by the accumulator fraction, tilts `root` to `groundNormal` (banks, ramps, slopes; eased, back toward level in the air, none on a loop) and plays a one-tick heading jump bigger than a normal turn (a wall's impact turn) out over a few frames. The chase camera follows `root`. The kart's secondary animation (anim.ts, next section) goes on `chassis` under it and on the model's morph targets. None of this touches the sim.

### View: the kart animation (render only, `anim.ts`)
Five video critiques (24 Sept 2026) said the karts stayed level through turns, with no body roll, suspension or squash, like rigid models sliding. `KartAnim` gives each kart springs driven by what its state does from tick to tick. It is stepped in `KartView.onTick` on the sim's 120 Hz tick, after the step, with the input the kart drove on, and interpolated per frame like the pose. So it looks the same at any frame rate, freezes with the hit-stop and slows with the finish slow-motion. It reads the kart state and the input and writes neither: anim.test.ts ticks it on deep-frozen states, and game/viewSim.test.ts races 8 animated karts to the flag on Harbour and Boardwalk and matches the bare sim (the leaderboard's replay loop, no views) byte for byte. The sim-math guard lists it as render-only. The same code runs for the player and the AI. A move over `teleport` 3 m in one tick (a set-down, a respawn) and a loop-the-loop or claw ride are not read as motion. Every number is in `KART_ANIM` (anim.ts); springs are frequency (Hz) and damping ratio (ζ).
- **Roll, lean-out.** The body leans out of a turn (the inside lifts) with its lateral acceleration (speed × yaw rate): `rollPerLatAccel` 0.0038 rad per m/s², at most `rollTurnMax` 0.09 (5°) in a grip turn (a full-lock turn at top speed is about 4.5°). A drift adds `driftRoll` 0.12 (9 to 11° in all, `rollMax` 0.21). None in the air or in a spin. A heading change over `turnPerTickMax` 0.03 rad in one tick is a wall's impact turn, not steering. Spring 2.4 Hz, ζ 0.5.
- **Drift yaw.** The schema's `driftVisualSlip` (0.22) plus `driftYawExtra` 0.14 × `drift.yawK` (the tighter the line, the further the tail hangs out). The tail flicks out at `driftKick` 2.2 rad/s as the drift locks, and during the hop the kart swivels `hopSwing` 0.12 toward the stick. Spring 3 Hz, ζ 0.55.
- **Pitch** (+ dips the nose): −`pitchPerAccel` 0.0055 rad per m/s² of forward acceleration (clamped to `accelClamp` 12 m/s² so a bump or a wall is not a nose-dive), within ±`pitchMax` 0.09. So the nose lifts accelerating and dips under hard braking. A boost's start kicks the nose up at `boostKick` 2 rad/s for a +30 % boost (more for a stronger one) and squats the rear onto its springs (`boostSquat` 2.4). A landing dips it at `landPitch` 0.1 rad/s per m/s of fall (up to `landCap` 14 m/s). Spring 2.6 Hz, ζ 0.45.
- **Suspension: squash and stretch, and the body on its springs.** One spring (4.2 Hz, ζ 0.36, at most `squashMax` 17 %) shows two ways: `squashShare` 0.6 of it as the whole kart's squash about its wheels' contact (volume kept: x and z scale 1/√(1 + s)), and the body sinking on its springs by `heavePerSquash` 0.55 m per unit (the rig's `heave`: the tyres stay on the road). A kick k peaks near 0.024 k, and no kick passes `kickMax` 6 (about 15 %), so a big landing peaks and never clips. Landing: −`landSquash` 1 per m/s of fall (a hop's landing about 8 %, anything from 6 m/s about 15 %), then it rebounds past rest. The road: −`bumpSquash` 0.4 per m/s that the ground's climb rate changes in one tick (capped at `bumpCap` 2.5), so it squashes at a rise's foot and stretches over a crest. The drift hop pops +`hopStretch` 3.4 (an 8 % stretch), a ramp's launch +`launchStretch` 0.5 per m/s of climb, a hit +`hitPop` 4.5.
- **Shoves.** A sideways speed change over `shoveMin` 1.5 m/s in one tick (a kart's bump, a wall, a bumper car; steering never makes one) jolts the kart: the body lags the push, so the side it is pushed toward lifts (`shoveRoll` 0.7 rad/s per m/s, capped at `shoveCap` 5), the driver's upper body jerks away (`shoveLean` 1), and it squashes (`shoveSquash` 0.4). A forward one (rear-ended, or ramming something; braking and boosts change speed far slower) lifts or dips the nose and the driver's head (`shovePitch` 0.35) and jolts it the same way.
- **Lift.** The chassis rides up by `halfTrack` × |sin roll| + `halfBase` × |sin pitch| (0.6 m each), so its low wheel stays on the road as it leans and the other side lifts.
- **Idle life.** A 9 Hz (`idleHz`) shiver at a standstill (`idleSquash` 0.5 %, fading out by `idleSpeed` 4 m/s). Revving on the grid shivers harder (`revSquash` 1.4 %) and squats the rear by `revSquat` 0.025 rad. Each kart's phase is its index times the golden angle, so the field is never in step.
- **Hits.** One whole turn (`spinTurns` 1) in the first `spinShare` 0.8 of the spin-out, timed by the sim's own `spinRemaining` and easing to a stop, plus a pop of stretch (plan §7.2 item 7). When the spin ends the whole turn is dropped from both ends of the interpolation at once, so it never unwinds on screen. While an oil slick or a tug slows the kart it fishtails (`slowWobble` 0.12 rad at `slowHz` 3). A trick flicks the chassis over and back (`trickFlick` 6 rad/s of roll, alternating sides).
- **Driver** (morph targets, art-pipeline rig.ts). It leans into the turn at `leanPerLatAccel` 0.01 rad per m/s², plus `leanDrift` 0.3 in a drift (at most `leanMax` 0.5), on a chassis leaning the other way, so in the world a drifting driver leans in about 17°. The head turns with the stick (`lookSteer` 0.3, into corners) and toward a drift (`lookDrift` 0.5, at most `lookMax` 0.55). It nods forward under braking and back under acceleration (`nodPerAccel` 0.009 per m/s², at most `nodMax` 0.14), and forward on landings (`nodLand` 0.36 rad/s per m/s) and bumps (`nodBump` 0.3). Springs: lean 2.2 Hz ζ 0.5, look 2 Hz ζ 0.75, nod 3 Hz ζ 0.38.
- **Front wheels** steer with the stick, `steerAngle` 0.38 rad (22°) at full lock (spring 7 Hz, ζ 0.85), and straighten in a spin. The wheels do not spin: the model files are one fused surface (a tyre is not a separate part), and at racing speed a turning wheel strobes anyway. (A racer built from parts rolls them: below.)
- **Reduced motion** (the game's `reduced` flag): everything × `reducedScale` 0.35, except the drift's slip and the wheels' steer, which say which way the kart is going. The hit's turn becomes a `spinWobble` 0.3 rad wobble.
- **On the chassis:** yaw first (slip, spin, wobble), then pitch and roll about the kart's own axes (Euler YXZ), the squash scale, then the lift. The driver, the wheels and the body's heave are the model's morph targets (influence = angle, or metres, over the rig's `rigUnit`). Nothing allocates per tick or frame, and no draw call is added. The chase camera follows `root`, so none of this moves it.
- **A racer built from parts** (art-pipeline rigged.ts: one skinned mesh, the kart's bones and the driver's 24) takes the same springs on its bones instead of morph targets (the body bone's heave, the front hubs' steer, the spine's lean, the neck and head's look and nod) plus its own animation (`driverAnim.ts`, `DRIVER_ANIM`, stepped on the same tick after KartAnim and interpolated the same way): the wheels roll at speed over their radius (capped at `spinMax` 28 rad/s so they never strobe backward at 60 fps; backward in reverse) and each bobs on its spring (`bobShare` 0.45 of its corner's lift, at most `bobMax` 6 cm); the steering wheel turns `wheelTurn` 1.3 rad at full lock and the hands go round with it (two-bone IK to the grips turned about the column); the head looks where `DriverContext` says (the camera on the grid for the player until 1.1 s before GO, over the line and on the podium; a rival within 3 m beside or 8 m just behind for `rival.hold` 1.15 s, then not again for 3 to 6 s; over the shoulder with the look-back button; small glances every 3.5 to 7.5 s on a straight), the neck and head up to `headMax` 1.25 rad and the spine's twist the rest (`twistMax` 0.5), springs with a top speed (`headSpeed` 6, `twistSpeed` 3 rad/s: a new look eases in, never whips round), never flipping across the back; the spine leans `throttleLean` 0.1 forward on the gas and `boostLean` 0.16 back on a boost, folds on landings, twists on a trick; the arms leave the wheel for a gesture (`armRate` 12/s blend) as aims in the torso's frame: a hit (both up, flailing, the head wobbling; back on the wheel `recover` 0.35 s after the spin), a trick (both up, the body twisting), an item (the right arm throws it forward, tosses it back for a drop or a look-back throw, or holds it up: 0.72 s), the finish reactions (fist pumps then a wave for the champion, a big wave for 2nd, fists up then a pump for 3rd, a brow wipe and a pump for relief, palms up with the shoulders for the shrug, hands kept on the wheel when deflated). With reduced motion the flourishes shrink and the look keeps its aim.

### What Mario Kart World actually does (researched 8 Sept 2026)
Sources: [Off-Road](https://www.mariowiki.com/Off-Road) and [MK8DX in-game statistics](https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart_8_Deluxe_in-game_statistics) on Super Mario Wiki, [MKWii boost data](https://wiki.mkwtas.com/wiki/Boost_information), [Vike's drifting guide](https://vikemk.com/drifting-guide), [Game8 MKW stats](https://game8.co/games/Mario-Kart-World/archives/523474), [VULKK on MKW stats](https://vulkk.com/2025/08/10/how-stats-work-in-mario-kart-world/). Mario Kart World itself is barely datamined as of Sept 2026, so the numbers below are MK8DX and MK Wii, and I say so where it matters.

- **Off-road is a speed cap, not grip.** The datamined `BrakeRt` for dirt is a flat fraction of top speed, in three tiers: 0.7 light, 0.5 medium, 0.3 heavy (deep sand). Steering, grip, drifting and mini-turbo charging are all *unaffected* by dirt. Our original grip-based off-road was wrong, hence the `gripOffroad` and `gripMud` change. We map dirt → 0.7 and mud → **0.6**, a softened medium tier (Adam, 8 Sept 2026), and leave 0.5 and 0.3 free for deeper surfaces later.
- **Only slippery ground cuts grip.** MK8DX handles ice and sand with a separate slip value near 0.9 of top speed plus real sliding. So ice keeps a modest speed cap (0.9) and pays in grip (2.5). That matches Frostbite Pass in the bible.
- **Boosts and air ignore the cap.** A dash panel in MK Wii carries explicit off-road immunity, and an airborne kart never touches the ground surface at all. This is why "hop over the mud" is a real Mario Kart line. We apply it to every boost source, one rule, easy to read.
- **Mini-turbo charge rate is not a weight thing.** MK8DX's hidden Mini-Turbo stat changes boost duration and strength, is set per character and kart part, and does not track weight at all: Bowser is at the top of the range and Wario is at the bottom, and both are heavy. So a "light karts charge faster" hook has no Mario Kart precedent. **Not added.**
- **Bump-by-weight is universal.** Every Mario Kart resolves a kart-to-kart bump with a collision mass, and Mario Kart World's own Weight tooltip says it "affects collision between vehicles". MK8DX also carries a separate larger mass while boosting. **Added**, as `dashMassBonus` plus mass-proportional separation.
- **Coins are a hit buffer, for everyone.** In Mario Kart a hit at zero coins spins you out; holding coins turns the same hit into a coin loss and a speed dip. Mario Kart World also cut coin loss from 3 to 2. **Added** as `base.coinShield` and `hitCoinsLost` 2. It replaces the invented "medium keeps coins" hook, which Adam removed from design §4 on 8 Sept 2026. Medium now has no hook, exactly as in Mario Kart World.
- **A class needs a verb, not a stat bar.** Mario Kart World removed inward drift and bikes stopped being interesting. The lesson for us: heavy has the bump, and that has to be genuinely felt.

Two boost numbers were off against the sources. Adam approved the change on 8 Sept 2026, so `docs/design.md` §7 and the schema now read:
| Was | Now | Source |
|---|---|---|
| drift boost +20% | **+30%** | MK Wii mini-turbo is +30%; the +20% row is its standstill mini-turbo, which plan §4.1 appears to have read by mistake |
| boost pad +30% for 1.0 s | **+40%** for 1.0 s | MK Wii dash panel, 60 frames |

Item speed is also +40%, so trick 1.3 < pad 1.4 = item 1.4 and the "boost never exceeds 1.4 × V" test still holds.

### Tests (headless, vitest, all deterministic)
1. Per-module tests in the table above.
2. **Determinism**: run a 3600-tick scripted log twice → byte-identical state; run it in two chunk sizes → identical.
3. **Drift tiers**: full stick from a standing drift fires tiers at 0.55 s, 1.33 s, 2.33 s ± 1 tick (0.83 / 1.83 / 2.83 before 24 Sept 2026).
4. **Boost cap**: over any log, `boost.multiplier ≤ 1.4` and `speed ≤ 1.4 × V_eff` where `V_eff` includes cc, archetype, surface and coins.
5. **Lap time**: scripted log on the flat oval stub finishes within ±2% of its recorded time. The Harbour Loop version of this test is added when track-builder lands and becomes the gate from then on.
6. **Archetype fairness**: the same AI drivers in light / medium / heavy finish a Time Trial within about 3% of each other on every real track at 100cc and 150cc (game/balance.e2e.test.ts: under 4% per track, under 1.5% on average); on the ovals heavy wins the long straights and light the tight bends.
7. **Surface cap**: driving onto mud at top speed settles at `0.6 × V` and takes about 0.4 s to bleed there, not one tick. Dirt settles at `0.7 × V`. Grip on dirt and mud is identical to road; only ice slides further.
8. **Cap bypass**: the same mud run with a live boost, and again with a hop over the patch, both keep full speed.
9. **Bump**: heavy into light moves the light kart further than light into heavy, and a boosting light kart wins the bump against a coasting heavy.
10. **Coin shield**: a hit with coins in hand slows and drops 2 coins with no spin; the same hit at zero coins spins for `hitSpinSeconds`.

## Lessons (repair loop writes here)
_(error → cause → fix → rule; newest first)_
- 2026-09-25: **Under the scripted near-perfect drifter, the heavy class and low-handling karts are the time-trial favorites** (the kart gate's first pass, game/combos.e2e.test.ts). In its own kart the heavy class was the fastest class on 5 of 6 tracks, 3.35% under the Frostbite median (−3.5% against medium, −4.1% against light). One handling step moved a Frostbite time by 3 to 4.6%, and G1 failed 138 of 480 pair-track checks (worst +4.64%, Gus in the Scrap Buggy). Cause: the drift charges at chargeFull (5 a frame) with the stick centred or into the drift and at chargeNeutral (3.5) with it pushed out. The drift turn is steerRate × lerp(driftSteerMin, driftSteerMax, yawK), so on the same line less handling needs more inward stick. It then charges at the full rate, reaches purple sooner and chains more mini-turbos through long bends: Frostbite bends 0 and 2 go 3-2 and 3-3-1 a lap for the heavy class against 3-0 for the light. More handling holds that line with the stick pushed out and charges slower. So in a drift, handling pays negative, against the +1/11 per step the kart model gives it. Fix for now: the kart gate measures with the Hard AI, the class gate's measure (docs/plans/kart-combos.md §3). The drifter's view is a report (game/combos.report.test.ts). The classes are Adam's and stay as they are. Proposed fix after 30 Sept (not built): charge from the drift's absolute curvature (its yaw rate over its speed) rather than the stick's side, so the same line charges at the same rate in every kart and handling only buys a tighter line. A rate relative to the kart's own turn limit (yawK) would keep the bias: more handling makes the same line a smaller share of a bigger limit. Rule: no stat may change how fast a drift charges on the same line. Check any charge or boost rule against the drifter's report across the three classes before tuning a kart.
- 2026-09-24: **CI failed three AI gates that passed on the Mac; an 8-AI race split at tick 522.** Cause: V8's Math.sin/cos/atan2/exp/tanh… return different last bits on arm64 and x64 (and Safari and Firefox differ again); ~40 ulp of AI steer grew into a different race, and the leaderboard server (Deno, x64) replays players' logs with the same sim. Fix: `src/sim-math/dmath.ts` (fdlibm in plain IEEE arithmetic) everywhere in the sim, a guard test, a golden bit hash. Rule: **sim code uses dmath, never Math transcendentals** (sqrt, abs, floor, round, min, max are exact and fine; no `**`, no Math.hypot).
- 2026-09-21: **The skid looked like it turned the wrong way.** Cause: `KartView` yawed the body by `−direction × driftVisualSlip`; the sim's positive yaw is screen-left, so a right drift showed the nose 28° to the left, out of the bend. The sim path was right all along (traced: heading and offset both go right). Fix: `+direction`, and 0.22 rad since the real slide already points the nose in. Rule: the same sign trap as the steering key; anything cosmetic that adds to `heading` follows the sim's sign, and a trace of the *sim* path settles whether a feel bug is physics or picture.
- 2026-09-21: **Driving nose-first into the edge parked the kart at 0 km/h.** Cause: the wall removed the outward part of the velocity, and with the nose square to the wall that was all of it; holding throttle just fed it back in. Fix: on a hard hit the nose swings `wallDeflect` (0.7) of the way toward the wall line and the remaining speed goes along it, so the kart slides on. Rule: a wall is something you glance off, never something you stick to; Mario Kart bounces, it never parks you.
- 2026-09-21: **Right arrow turned the kart left on screen.** Cause: `rightOf(heading)` is `up × forward`, which in Three's right-handed Y-up frame is the *left*; every sim-side "right" (lateral +, steer +, drift direction +1) is screen-left. The sim, track lateral, race-manager and AI are all consistent with each other, so nothing there changes. Fix: `mapInput` gives the right key and stick −1. Rule: sim "right" means the `rightOf` vector, not the screen; anything that meets a human (input, HUD arrows, minimap) flips at the boundary, in one place.
- 2026-09-15: Spin ended with speed 1e-15, not 0 → `spinRemaining` after 120 ticks of 1/120 was 1e-15, not 0, so the decay ratio was tiny but nonzero → `countDown` snaps anything under 1e-9 s to 0 → rule: every countdown timer needs an epsilon snap; never compare a float timer to exactly 0 without one.
- 2026-09-15: Boost surface never fired when landing onto it → the surface check ran before the ground snap, so `grounded` was still false, then the next tick saw `surface === 'boost'` already and skipped the edge → moved the check after the snap and fire on `!wasGrounded` too → rule: anything that keys off "entered surface X" must run after the ground step and treat landing as an entry.
- 2026-09-15: Track `gripScale` was read into the sample and thrown away → steer (step 5) runs before ground (step 8), so the sample was not available yet → store `gripScale` on the kart in the ground step and read it next tick → rule: any per-tick track value the early steps need is cached on the kart by the ground step, one tick late, on purpose.
- 2026-09-14: Drift-tier timing test found no tiers → the test kart held full lock on the 16 m wide oval, hit the wall every tick, `wallScrub` bled it under `driftKeepSpeed` and the drift cancelled → run full-lock drift tests on a wide stub (`halfWidth: 60`) → rule: a test that holds a steady drift needs room for the circle; the sim was right.
- 2026-09-14: Hop landed at 0.20 s, not `hopSeconds` 0.25 → `groundStick` 0.12 m catches the kart on the way down (peak height is only 0.2 m) → test accepts 0.7×–1.0× hopSeconds → rule: `groundStick` shortens every hop; the landing window for locking a drift is 2 × hopSeconds so this never costs a drift.
- 2026-09-23: **A hop over the loop's catch line skipped the ride and saved 1.7 s a lap (bug review).** Cause: the catch was a one-tick line checked with last tick's `grounded`; a kart in the air on that tick was never checked again, and drove through the ring. Fix: the run-in is a zone; any grounded tick inside it, moving forward, starts the ride at the metres already covered (`loopS0`). Rule: a set piece that must catch every kart catches over a stretch of road, never on one crossing tick, and checks the direction of travel.
- 2026-09-23: **A kart shoved behind a ramp lip was trapped by the lip wall (regression test).** Cause: kart-kart pushes move a kart after its t was taken, so last tick's t was stale and every tick looked like a backwards crossing. Fix: near a lip, measure where the kart was again (`nearest` on the pre-move position). Rule: any rule that compares this tick's t with last tick's must re-measure the old t if anything moves karts between ticks.
- 2026-09-23: **Ramps lifted karts on open shoulders where no ramp is drawn.** Cause: `jumpLift` ignored lateral. Fix: a ramp spans kerb to kerb (lift and launch only for |lateral| ≤ halfWidth); bumps round off with `edgeTaper`. Rule: every physics shape matches its mesh at the edges too.
- 2026-09-25: **The podium's three winners vanished in a real Grand Prix (found by a whole cup run through the real game).** Cause: the podium ticks its KartViews on the frame's time, and anim.ts divides by dt: one 0 s frame (paused, hidden, a warm-up's late clock, a dev step) put NaN in the springs for good. Fix: a tick of no time returns at once. Rule: anything that divides by a time step guards dt > 0, and anything fed a render dt is tested with a 0 s frame.
