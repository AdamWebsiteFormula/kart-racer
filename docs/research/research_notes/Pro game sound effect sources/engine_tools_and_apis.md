# Engine-Sound Tools, Vehicle Recordings, and Sound-Generation APIs for a Web Kart Racer

## Engine tools: Krotos Igniter/Studio, Crankcase Audio REV, Wwise/FMOD vehicle packs, and whether rendered output can ship as MP3 in a web game

### Takeaway
All the DAW-plugin and middleware engine-sound tools (Krotos Igniter, Krotos Studio, Crankcase Audio REV) are built to be *performed and rendered inside a host* (a DAW, or a game engine's audio middleware); none of them advertise a browser/Web Audio/Three.js target. For "Rascal Rally!" (Web Audio, MP3 loops, no Wwise/FMOD/Unity/Unreal), the realistic path is to use these tools (or raw recordings) to author/record the source material, bounce/export to WAV in the DAW, then encode to MP3 yourself — standard practice, but not a workflow any of these vendors document explicitly, and none of the three vendor pages fetched stated an explicit commercial-game redistribution license for the rendered audio.

### Cited Findings
- **Krotos Igniter** costs $399 as currently listed (sale-adjusted) — [Krotos Igniter product page](https://www.krotosaudio.com/igniter/); a 2025 product-announcement source lists regular pricing at "$599 / £449" — [ask.video coverage](https://ask.video/article/audio-software/krotos-audio-unveils-igniter-complete-vehicle-sound-fx-design-solution); Krotos is currently running "The Stop Waiting Sale" advertising "up to 70% off" its catalogue including Igniter — [Krotos Sale 2026 page](https://www.krotosaudio.com/the-stop-waiting-sale-2026/). (The $399 vs $599 discrepancy is likely sale vs. list price; not fully reconciled this session.)
- Igniter is a hybrid procedural + sample-based tool combining "Granular, Synth, One Shot or Loop modules," with 24 performable vehicle presets (cars, motorcycles, helicopters, aircraft, drones) — [Krotos Igniter product page](https://www.krotosaudio.com/igniter/).
- **Igniter Full Tank** (Igniter plus an extra sound-library collection) costs $999 — [Krotos Igniter FAQ: Igniter vs Full Tank](https://www.krotosaudio.com/support/faq/what-is-the-difference-between-igniter-and-igniter-full-tank/).
- Igniter offers a **10-day free trial**, and ships as AAX Native/VST/AU (64-bit only) for Pro Tools, Logic Pro X, Nuendo, Cubase, Reaper, and Ableton Live 10+ — [Krotos Igniter product page](https://www.krotosaudio.com/igniter/). No export-format or commercial-license wording for rendered output was found on the page.
- **Krotos Studio (Pro)** is a separate, newer product line: a desktop app/plugin for real-time performable SFX inside Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, or a DAW, including combustion and electric car "rev" presets — [Krotos Studio](https://krotos.studio/) and [Sound on Sound review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/krotos-studio).

Pricing for the pack itself was not disclosed on that page.
- Krotos Studio Pro pricing (from secondary retail listings, not confirmed on krotos.studio directly): annual subscription reported at both **$195.99/year** and **$279.99/year** (with a $265.99 discounted price at one reseller), and roughly **$47/month** on monthly billing; a 5-day free trial is offered, no credit card required — [B&H Photo listing](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1930015-REG/krotos_audio_ka_stud_pro_pro_annual_subscription.html), [MotionMedia listing](https://www.motionmedia.com/krotos-studio-pro-annual-subscription/) (figures conflict between sources; treat as approximate).
- **Crankcase Audio REV** works "in the frequency domain" for "bi-directional and variable speed playback of recorded accelerations," using granular/spectral-analysis techniques that eliminate traditional looping — [Crankcase Audio homepage](https://www.crankcaseaudio.com/) and [Audiokinetic Wwise plugin page for REV](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/wwise/plugins/crankcase-rev-plugin) ("Create realistic engine sounds using granular synthesis techniques to eliminate looping").
- REV's runtime is "platform agnostic," having shipped on PC, Mac, iPhone, Android, Xbox, and PlayStation, with official middleware integrations for **Wwise** (partnered plugin) and **FMOD** (DSP plugin), a native **Unreal Engine** implementation, Audio Units (iPhone) and DirectX support, plus community Unity implementations — [Crankcase Audio homepage](https://www.crankcaseaudio.com/); [REV Unity Tachometer Demo (GitHub)](https://github.com/CrankcaseAudio/CrankcaseAudioREVUnity). No browser/Web Audio/Three.js integration is listed anywhere.
- REV pricing is quoted "on a per-title basis"; exact figures are not published and require contacting the vendor (support@crankcaseaudio.com) — [Crankcase Audio homepage](https://www.crankcaseaudio.com/).
- Vehicle-specific **Wwise/FMOD sample packs** do exist as commercial asset-store products, e.g. "Motorbike Engine Sounds Vol. 1" (12 looping motorbike engines, ships with a pre-made FMOD sound bank and Unity/RES2 compatibility) and "Italian V8 Engine Sound" (same FMOD-bank + Unity-RES2 pattern) on GameDev Market, and "Engine Sounds SFX Pack Vol. 2" on the Unreal Marketplace (ships with an FMOD project) — [Motorbike Engine Sounds Vol. 1](https://www.gamedevmarket.net/asset/motorbike-engine-sounds-vol-1), [Italian V8 Engine Sound](https://www.gamedevmarket.net/asset/v8-italian-engine-sound-pack), [Engine Sounds SFX Pack Vol. 2](https://www.unrealengine.com/marketplace/en-US/product/engine-sounds-sfx-pack-vol-2). These are fundamentally WAV loop libraries with an optional middleware bank layered on top — the raw WAVs are directly reusable outside Wwise/FMOD.
- No go-kart-specific Wwise or FMOD sample pack was found in this search (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- For a Web Audio/Three.js game with no Wwise/FMOD/Unity/Unreal runtime, Krotos Igniter and Krotos Studio are usable only as **authoring tools**: perform/design the engine sound in the DAW or app, bounce to WAV, then convert to MP3 with any standard encoder (e.g., ffmpeg) for use as a Web Audio buffer. Nothing found on either product's pages restricts this downstream use, but neither explicitly confirms it either — treat the "can I ship the bounced audio in my own commercial game" question as needing a direct EULA read before relying on it.
- Crankcase REV is the weakest fit of the three for this project specifically because its entire value proposition (real-time granular resynthesis at runtime) is delivered through a runtime plugin for engines Rascal Rally! doesn't use; extracting static loops from it would mean running it inside one of its supported hosts and capturing the output externally — an undocumented, awkward workflow, not a first-class export path.
- The Wwise/FMOD-bundled sample packs found are best understood as ordinary **WAV sound libraries** with bonus middleware banks; ignore the FMOD/Wwise packaging and use the loops directly.

### Gaps
- No explicit statement, on any of the three vendor sites, of a license permitting redistribution of *rendered/bounced audio* inside a shipped commercial or free public game (as opposed to using the plugin itself under its software EULA). This should be confirmed directly against each vendor's EULA before committing to a purchase.
- Whether Igniter/Krotos Studio impose any per-seat/per-project restrction on *exported audio* specifically (vs. plugin usage) was not found.
- No go-kart-specific (as opposed to car/motorbike) Wwise or FMOD sample pack was located.
- Exact current (non-sale) list price of Igniter was not fully reconciled ($399 vs. $599 across sources).

## APIs and generators: Freesound, Stable Audio, Adobe Firefly, ElevenLabs, Meta AudioGen/AudioCraft, TangoFlux, MMAudio and other 2026 generators

### Takeaway
Every major 2026 text-to-SFX generator can output MP3/WAV directly usable in Web Audio with no DAW or game engine needed, but licensing and cost models vary sharply: Freesound's *API* is non-commercial-only by default (commercial requires a negotiated license), Meta's AudioGen/AudioCraft pretrained weights are non-commercial (CC-BY-NC 4.0) and impractical to self-host under a deadline, while Stable Audio, ElevenLabs, Adobe Firefly, and MMAudio all offer paid commercial-use tiers. Reviewer consensus (from general AI-tool roundups, not specialist game-audio press) currently calls ElevenLabs the SFX quality leader — which sits in tension with Adam's own verdict that the ElevenLabs sounds he generated were "cheap" and "terrible," suggesting the gap is specific to convincing multi-RPM engine loops rather than SFX generation in general.

### Cited Findings

- The official API Terms of Use state plainly: "You can use the Freesound API for free only for non-commercial purposes," with commercial terms "negotiated on a case by case basis with UPF [Universitat Pompeu Fabra], including limits regarding the volume of use of the Platform" — [Freesound API Terms of Use](https://freesound.org/docs/api/terms_of_use.html) and [Freesound API ToS (full)](https://freesound.org/help/tos_api/).

## What game-audio professionals say makes kart/arcade racing engine audio sound good

### Takeaway
The professional consensus (drawn from a 2014 Designing Sound piece synthesizing GDC-adjacent talks on Project CARS/Forza/REV, and BOOM Library's car-engine sound-design primer) is that convincing engine audio comes from either (a) many crossfaded RPM-layer loops per mic perspective driven by physics parameters, or (b) granular synthesis of real recordings — and that correctly handling the on-throttle/off-throttle transition matters as much as the loop layering itself. This maps directly onto Rascal Rally's stated approach ("crossfading RPM loops" in Web Audio), and BOOM Library's primer gives concrete, actionable numbers for how many loops and at what RPM spacing.

### Cited Findings
- **Project CARS** used "well over one hundred separate wave files that together constitute a 'sound set'" per audio director Stephen Baysted, layered and crossfaded and driven by physics inputs — [Designing Sound: Vehicle Engine Design — Project CARS, Forza Motorsport 5 and REV](https://designingsound.org/2014/08/11/vehicle-engine-design-project-cars-forza-motorsport-5-and-rev/).
- **Forza Motorsport 5** used "hundreds of physics-based parameters to drive the audio system," including independently tracked RPM for engine/turbo/supercharger plus throttle position, engine load, torque, boost pressure, clutch position, and gear selection, per Audio Creative Director Nick Wiswell — same source.
- Veteran vehicle-audio designer **Greg Hill** (18+ years, 80+ titles) frames the two dominant implementation methods as: **crossfaded loops** ("with a crossfaded system I can take the best parts from a range of source and provide very realistic results," noting traditional loop modeling takes "3 to 4 days" per engine) and **granular synthesis** (spectral analysis reassembled at runtime) — same source.
- Crankcase Audio's **REV** (built by Adam Boyd and John Twigg) reduces the granular-modeling turnaround to about **10 minutes** by tracking harmonic components and slicing recordings "at the granularity of individual pistons firing," and includes "a layered load/offload mixing system" — same source.
- Nick Wiswell frames the on/off-throttle question as central: "How does the sound character shift between on and off throttle? Does positive or negative engine load impact this?" — same source.
- BOOM Library's **"Car Engine Sound Primer"** (Mike Caviezel) recommends, for loop-based (crossfade) systems, roughly **13 loops per engine perspective**, sampled "every 500 RPM or so" through the rev range, with denser sampling ("every 250 RPM") at low RPM — [BOOM Library: The Car Engine Sound Primer](https://www.boomlibrary.com/blog/the-car-engine-sound-primer-mike-caviezel/).
- The primer stresses **on-throttle vs. off-throttle** handling: off-throttle recordings should have the intake sound removed entirely, engine-layer volume reduced, and the exhaust EQ'd to "match the real-world off-throttle timbre" — same source.
- Comparing methods: **granular** needs only 3 source files (an acceleration ramp, a deceleration ramp, and an idle loop) and auto-handles load switching, at the cost of higher CPU use; **loop-based** needs 52+ files (13 loops × 4 mic perspectives) but is cheaper on CPU and needs "creative DSP workarounds for throttle response" — same source.
- Recommended onboard recording perspectives: exhaust (tailpipe mics), engine bay, intake air noise, and interior/cabin acoustics; "the exhaust note is the primary character of a car's sound" — same source.
- Core implementation rule stated directly: "Ensure that the audible Engine Audio RPM matches the Game Physics Engine RPM" and "Engine Audio react[s] to Load (Throttle) in a believable way" — same source.
- Secondary (unverified against the primary GDC Vault talk, which is paywalled) reporting describes **GTA V's RAGE engine** using "a granular engine to synthesize the player's car" with additional oscillators synced to the granular clock to add sub-harmonic frequencies (referencing a GDC talk by Alastair MacGregor), and **The Crew 2** using granular synthesis to cut engine recordings into "a multitude of loops which could then be recomposed" (referencing Ubisoft's Nathan Blias) — both via [WebSearch synthesis of GDC-adjacent sources]; the original GDC Vault pages exist but are access-gated: [GDC Vault: Granular Synthesis in Next-Generation Games](https://gdcvault.com/play/1013205/Granular-Synthesis-in-Next-Generation), [GDC Vault: Data Driven Granular Synthesis for Video Games](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023987/Data-Driven-Granular-Synthesis-for).

### Inferences
- Rascal Rally's stated approach — crossfading RPM loops in Web Audio — is exactly the "loop-based" method described by Greg Hill and detailed by BOOM Library, so the actionable takeaway is concrete: aim for **roughly 13 RPM loops per mic perspective** (denser than every 500 RPM at the low end, e.g. every 250 RPM), record or source **separate on-throttle and off-throttle variants**, and make sure whatever engine-RPM value drives the kart-controller physics is the exact value used to select/crossfade loops (matching "audible RPM must match physics RPM").
- Given the kinematic (non-physics-engine) kart controller described in this project's architecture rules, a full physics-parameter system like Forza's is out of scope, but the throttle-state distinction (on/off) is cheap to add and is called out by every professional source found as one of the most important perceptual cues — worth prioritizing over adding more RPM layers.
- Granular synthesis (REV-style) is the higher-fidelity, lower-authoring-effort option in principle, but per the Engine-tools section above, there's no clean way to run REV inside a browser/Web Audio pipeline — so the loop-crossfade method isn't just the simpler choice for this project, it may be the only practical one given the stack.
