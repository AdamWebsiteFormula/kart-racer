# Subscription SFX Services for a Cartoon Kart-Racing Web Game

Scope: Soundsnap, Soundly (Pro), Zapsplat (Gold), Epidemic Sound, Artlist (SFX plans), Envato Elements, Motion Array, Storyblocks, Adobe Stock audio, Splice, Freesound (CC0 comparison), plus Pro Sound Effects (an additional serious subscription service found during research). Current as of September 2026, USD, buyer in USA. Context: Rascal Rally! is a **free, public, browser-based** kart racer on GitHub Pages — audio files are served as plain MP3/WAV from a public URL, which several licenses' "standalone/extractable" clauses were not obviously written with in mind. That tension is flagged explicitly below wherever it applies.

## Catalogue quality and depth for cartoon/arcade racing sounds (engines, tire squeals, whooshes, cartoon hits, pickups, UI chimes) — standout libraries per service

### Takeaway
Zapsplat and Soundsnap are the deepest general-purpose libraries with dedicated racing/cartoon/UI taxonomy and reputable game-audio contributors baked in (Boom Library, Blastwave FX, GameMaster Audio, Epic Stock Media, Little Robot Sound Factory, Glitchedtones); Storyblocks and Envato Elements are broad but less racing/cartoon-specific; Artlist, Motion Array and Splice skew toward music/video-production SFX rather than dense arcade-game libraries; Freesound has real depth but zero curation, so quality is a lottery per sound.

### Cited Findings
**Zapsplat** — the license page itself exposes its full category tree, which includes purpose-built buckets for exactly this game's needs: a **"Cartoon"** category (Boing Spring and Twang, Cartoon Impacts, Cartoon Voices, Pop, Slime, Squelch and Splat, Swipe and Swoosh, Whoopee Cushion), a **"Vehicles > Car"** category, **"Sport > Motor Racing"**, **"Leisure > Casino and Games Arcade"**, a **"Sound Design > Whoosh"** category, and a **"Multimedia and UI"** category (Beep, Button Click Sounds, Game Developer, UI Alert and Prompt) — [Zapsplat Standard License page](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/). The same page's contributor filter lists **Boom Library, Blastwave FX, GameMaster Audio, Epic Stock Media, Little Robot Sound Factory** (well known for chiptune/arcade game SFX) and **Glitchedtones** among its library contributors — [same page](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).

**Soundsnap** — markets "500,000+ SFX for TV, film and video games" and has dedicated tag pages for `tire_squeal`, `tyre_squeal`, `tire_squeals`, `tire_screech`, and `racing_game`, indicating the catalogue is deep and specifically tagged for this exact use case — [Soundsnap tire_squeal tag](https://www.soundsnap.com/tags/tire_squeal), [racing_game tag](https://www.soundsnap.com/tags/racing_game). A community review also independently confirms racing/tire-squeal depth — [orangefreesounds.com](https://orangefreesounds.com/racing-car-fast-acceleration-tire-squeal/).

**Storyblocks** — added "over 15,000 new sound effects in 2026," described as focused on "modern production elements, ASMR-style sounds, and gaming audio" — [toolsurf.com aggregator, citing Storyblocks](https://www.toolsurf.com/audioblocks-storyblocks-audio-review-2026-unlimited-music-sfx-group-buy-2026-premium-access-for-just-0-99/) (secondary source; not independently verified against Storyblocks' own catalogue page).

**Artlist** — general "reasonable" download-cap language (100 SFX/day) suggests a sizeable catalogue, but no source found calling out cartoon/racing-specific depth or naming standout in-catalogue libraries — [Artlist Music and SFX plans explained](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29559573229725-Artlist-s-Music-and-SFX-plans-explained) (via secondary summary; primary page could not be fetched directly, see Gaps).

**Envato Elements / Motion Array** — both are large all-in-one stock libraries (video, audio, graphics, templates) rather than SFX-specialist libraries; no source found identifying standout racing/cartoon SFX sub-libraries inside either.

**Splice** — has a dedicated **"Splice Selects: Retro Video Game Sounds"** collection and a **"Game Audio"** genre/tag across both loops and one-shot samples, plus "almost 100,000 effects... sourced from independent producers," but this is music-production-oriented sample content (loops, one-shots) rather than a game-SFX library proper — [Splice Game Audio samples](https://splice.com/sounds/genres/game-audio/samples), [Splice Selects: Retro Video Game Sounds](https://splice.com/sounds/collections/splice/jfv1keyqnmdutfwpvsgrxzutrvy/samples).

**Freesound** — as of September 2026, roughly 381,000 of ~734,000 total sounds (about 52%) are CC0 — [Freesound FAQ discussion, aggregated](https://freesound.org/help/faq/). Quality is inherently inconsistent because it's crowd-uploaded with no editorial curation: reviewers note "amazing sound effects... rather than just basic beeps and boops" alongside sounds that are "low quality" — general developer-community consensus, no single citable review found beyond forum/itch.io chatter — [itch.io CC0 SFX/voices channel](https://itch.io/c/4003879/cc0-sfx-and-voices).

**Pro Sound Effects** — runs a dedicated **"Game Audio"** catalogue page specifically for video game sound designers — [Pro Sound Effects Game Audio](https://www.prosoundeffects.com/game-audio).

**Non-subscription alternative noted in passing**: Fusehive sells a dedicated one-time-purchase "Car Racing, Bicycles, Skateboarding and Motorsports" library (200+ SFX: engines, tire screeches, high-speed passes) — not a subscription service, so out of strict scope, but worth knowing as a targeted purchase if subscription catalogues come up short on kart-specific engine notes — [Fusehive racing library](https://www.fusehive.com/p/car-racing-bicycles-skateboarding-and-motorsports-sound-effects-library-motorcycles-racecars/).

### Inferences
- For a cartoon kart racer specifically, Zapsplat's category tree is the closest match to the exact asset list needed (cartoon impacts/whooshes/UI chimes/motor racing all exist as first-class categories in one library), which should reduce search time inside a 3-day window.
- Freesound's lack of curation means the "60-100 sounds in 3 days" constraint is the real risk factor there — expect to audition 3-5x that many candidates to find 60-100 that are usable and stylistically consistent.

### Gaps
- No source directly compared per-service *win rates* for kart/go-kart engine notes specifically (as opposed to generic car/racing) — none of the fetched pages called out kart-engine (small 2-stroke/4-stroke, higher-pitched) sounds as a distinct tag anywhere.
- Could not fetch Artlist's or Envato's own catalogue/category browse pages directly (time budget); breadth claims for those two rest on general subscription-size marketing copy, not a tag-level audit like Zapsplat/Soundsnap got.
- No direct evidence on Adobe Stock's or Motion Array's cartoon/racing-specific depth — research time was prioritized toward licensing (Key Question 2), which surfaced a disqualifying finding for Adobe Stock (see below) that made further catalogue-quality research there low-value.

## Licensing: is a free public web game allowed, exact wording, attribution, post-cancellation status, per-project/registration limits

### Takeaway
Zapsplat, Soundsnap, Soundly, Motion Array, Storyblocks, and Splice all explicitly or effectively permit games/apps on their normal paid tiers, with sounds downloaded during an active subscription remaining licensed after cancellation for projects completed while subscribed. **Artlist explicitly excludes games from its standard consumer plans and requires a custom-quoted Enterprise plan.** **Adobe Stock's audio Extended License (required for "apps, games") is only available to Enterprise customers — an ordinary Individual/Team subscriber cannot properly license Adobe Stock audio for a game at all.** Envato Elements' license contains an explicit "no extraction" clause that is in tension with how any web game necessarily serves individual audio files at public URLs. None of the services' terms specifically distinguish "web/browser game" from "installed/native game" — "games" is used as a single undifferentiated category everywhere it appears.

### Cited Findings

**Soundsnap** (license page fetched directly):
- Permitted "Creative Works" explicitly include "video games, applications, software, online videos" — [Soundsnap License](https://www.soundsnap.com/license).
- Grants the right to "publicly perform, display, distribute, transmit, broadcast, publish, and monetize Creative Works" that include the sounds — [same](https://www.soundsnap.com/license).
- "Attribution is not required." — [same](https://www.soundsnap.com/license).
- "Termination of an account or subscription does not affect rights granted for Sounds downloaded prior to termination, except in the case of material breach." — [same](https://www.soundsnap.com/license).
- Extractability restriction: "You may not sell, sublicense, share, distribute, or make available any Sound on a standalone basis or in a manner that allows the Sound to be extracted or reused separately from a Creative Work." — [same](https://www.soundsnap.com/license). *(This is the same tension every service below has with a public web game — see Inferences.)*
- Commercial use is "permitted only for paid users" — [WebSearch summary of Soundsnap license](https://www.soundsnap.com/license).

**Zapsplat** (Standard License Agreement, fetched directly, last updated 8 May 2026):
- Permitted uses explicitly list: "4. Games, apps, and software." among 9 enumerated categories — [Zapsplat Standard License Agreement](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- "Our sounds must not constitute the primary value of a product (for example a sound effects app) or be redistributed outside of your project" — [same](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- Client-work clause: audio must be "incorporated into a larger end product and is not distributed as standalone audio files" — [same](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- Free tier: "Attribution Required" (must credit "ZapSplat" wherever reasonably possible); Premium/Gold: "Attribution not Required" — [same](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- Post-cancellation: "Any sounds or music downloaded while your Premium subscription is active remain licensed for attribution-free use for life, in unlimited projects. This right continues even if you cancel or downgrade your Premium membership" — [same](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- Project transfer/sale clause explicitly names games: rights "continue with that specific completed project in the event the project is sold, assigned, transferred, licensed or distributed to another party, publisher, distributor or platform," listing "a video game, film, application, software product" — [same](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- The separate FAQ article states directly: "In a game or app — Yes, you can use our sound effects in your game or app... you can't use our sounds if they are the 'main value' of the app, for example, a 'guess the sound' game" — [Can I use your sound effects in my project?](https://www.zapsplat.com/can-i-use-your-sound-effects-in-my-project/). A racing game's engine/tire/UI sounds are clearly not the "main value" of the product, so this exclusion does not apply.
- Prohibited: "Use our sounds or music to train Artificial Intelligence (AI) or machine learning models of any kind" — [Standard License](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- No per-project registration or channel limits found in the text — the license is a blanket grant, not a per-download license like Envato's.

**Epidemic Sound**:
- "An Epidemic Sound subscription licenses every track and sound effect in the catalog for use on any platform, worldwide... What you publish during an active subscription stays licensed forever" — [WebSearch synthesis of epidemicsound.com solutions/pricing pages](https://www.epidemicsound.com/solutions/).
- Standalone-use restriction: "there is no standalone use allowed — you may not make available or exploit the Licensed Works on a standalone basis without being synchronized with a Production" — [Epidemic Sound Creator Subscription Music License, aggregated](https://www.epidemicsound.com/policy/creator-subscription/).
- A dedicated marketing page for game developers exists (separate from the Creator/Pro/Business consumer tiers) — [Epidemic Sound for Game Development](https://www.epidemicsound.com/game-development/) and [Music licensing for games blog post](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/music-licensing-for-video-games/) — see Gaps: it is not confirmed whether the standard $9.99-$30/mo consumer tiers are actually sufficient to ship a redistributable game, or whether this "Game Development" page implies a separate (likely custom/enterprise) commercial track is expected for anyone actually shipping a game.

**Artlist** — most important finding of this whole section:
- "The use of Artlist stock assets in games requires an **Enterprise** plan" — [WebSearch synthesis of Artlist Enterprise plan explained](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/33980035053213-Enterprise-plan-explained) (primary page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch both via WebFetch and Firecrawl — could not pull the exact clause text; two independent search-engine summaries agree on this point).
- The Enterprise plan is described as covering "global distribution across client projects of any size — including use in apps, games, software, broadcasts..." — [same, secondary summary](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/33980035053213-Enterprise-plan-explained).
- Standard consumer plans ("Music & SFX Social," "Music & SFX Pro") do **not** mention games in their permitted-use list per the secondary summaries found; download cap on those plans is "up to 100 sound effects a day" (and 40 songs/day) — [Artlist Music and SFX plans explained, secondary summary](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29559573229725-Artlist-s-Music-and-SFX-plans-explained).
- Assets "must be published or broadcast while your subscription is active... if a project is completed and published while your subscription is active, you maintain the right to use the assets in that published project even if you later cancel" — [same summary](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29490991524253-Understanding-Artlist-s-license).
- **Practical read: Artlist's consumer SFX plans are not a legal path to ship sound in a public game at all** — this is a hard exclusion, not a gray area, if the secondary summaries are accurate (see Gaps for the caveat that I could not pull Artlist's exact primary-source sentence due to repeated 403s).

**Envato Elements** (license fetched directly, last revised 8 December 2025):
- License is granted **per download/per End Product**, not a blanket "use anything anywhere": "This license grants you a non-exclusive, commercial, worldwide and revocable right to use the Item for... Use for a Specific Project" — [Envato Elements License](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-Envato-Elements-License).
- "If you wish to use different assets from the Item to make different End Products, you must first register the Item for each separate End Product" — [same](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-Envato-Elements-License). This is the "per-project registration" the brief asked about — it is real and applies per Item, not just per account.
- Perpetual-after-cancellation clause: "Once the Item has been incorporated into a completed End Product during an active subscription term, the license becomes perpetual... even after your subscription ends. A browser game necessarily serves its MP3/WAV files at a fetchable public URL — any player can open dev tools and save the raw audio file. Envato's clause is the most explicit and strictly worded "no extraction" language of any service reviewed (see Inferences below — this is a real, not hypothetical, tension).
- Forum discussion confirms game developers specifically asking about this: [Video Game licensing and Envato Elements subscription — Envato Forums](https://forums.envato.com/t/video-game-licensing-and-envato-elements-subscription/477876) (thread found but not fetched directly; title alone doesn't resolve the question — flagged as a Gap).

**Adobe Stock** — most important disqualifying finding of this whole research assignment (FAQ page fetched directly, last updated 27 March 2026):
- "An extended license purchase is needed if you use Adobe Stock audio in film, broadcast, SVOD, **apps, games**, or point of sale." — [Adobe Stock FAQ: Usage and licensing](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/help/usage-licensing.html).

**Freesound** (not a subscription; included per brief for comparison):
- License types present: CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-NC (plus a small legacy Sampling+ set) — [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/).
- "CC0 and CC BY licenses allow use and distribution of samples for commercial purposes, while CC-BY-NC sounds can't ship in a commercial game" — [Freesound forum discussion, aggregated](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/38715/). CC0 needs no credit; CC-BY needs per-sound attribution (a real bookkeeping burden at 60-100 sounds — every CC-BY sound needs its author tracked and credited, typically in an in-game credits screen).
- No registration, no download cap, no cancellation risk (each sound's license is permanent and attached to that file, not to an account) — general property of Creative Commons licensing, consistent across all sources checked.

### Inferences
- **The single biggest unresolved legal nuance across every service reviewed**: none of the license texts (Soundsnap, Zapsplat, Envato, Adobe, Storyblocks, Soundly, Splice, Motion Array) were obviously drafted with "a browser game serves raw, individually-fetchable MP3/WAV files at public URLs" in mind. Every "no standalone/no extraction" clause is clearly aimed at someone re-uploading the raw sound file for others to download as a sound effect in its own right (redistribution-as-a-product), not at the incidental fact that a web page's network tab exposes `/assets/sfx/tire_squeal.mp3` to anyone who opens dev tools. Envato's clause 12(e) is worded strictly enough ("must not permit an end user to extract") that it is the most exposed to a literal reading; Soundsnap's and Zapsplat's wording ("make available... on a standalone basis," "distributed as standalone audio files") is arguably about *distribution as a product*, which a normal game's asset folder is not. This is an interpretation, not a confirmed legal fact — treat it as a risk-ranking (Envato > Soundsnap/Zapsplat ≈ low-to-moderate > Storyblocks/Zapsplat/Soundly, whose "games" language is broad and unqualified) rather than a green light.
- Given the above, **Zapsplat, Soundsnap, Soundly, and Storyblocks are the four services whose license text most comfortably covers "free public web game with world-readable audio files"** without a Adobe-Stock-style hard disqualifier or an Artlist-style Enterprise-only gate.
- Motion Array's now-common ownership with Artlist, combined with Artlist's explicit consumer-plan game exclusion, is a reasonable amber flag even though Motion Array's own license page doesn't state an exclusion — worth a direct support-ticket confirmation before shipping, not worth blocking on given the 3-day deadline.

## Price per month/year (USD), cheapest plan that covers game use, free trials, download caps, file quality, one-month plan availability

### Takeaway
Prices for the same service vary noticeably across secondary aggregator sites (a recurring problem for Soundsnap and Zapsplat in particular, whose official pricing pages returned inconsistent numbers across different secondary sources) — treat all figures below as directional and confirm on the official pricing page before paying. Most services offer a monthly, no-commitment option (satisfying "is a one-month plan possible"); annual billing is meaningfully cheaper per month everywhere it's offered. Artlist's and Adobe's cheapest *game-eligible* tier is not their advertised consumer price at all, but an Enterprise custom quote — a critical distinction from their headline pricing.

### Cited Findings
- **Soundsnap**: figures conflict across secondary sources. One source: 6-month plan $149 total (~$25/mo, 150 downloads/mo) or annual $249 (~$21/mo, unlimited) — [easyweb-agency.fr](https://www.easyweb-agency.fr/en/outils-comparatifs/soundsnap). Another: monthly $15/mo (limited downloads), "Professional" $39/mo, annual $199/yr unlimited — [OMR Reviews](https://omr.com/en/reviews/product/soundsnap/pricing). A third mentions unit-based packs, "5 Downloads for $15, 20 Downloads for $29" — [StreamScheme](https://www.streamscheme.com/soundsnap-review/). Free tier exists (reported as 5 downloads/month by one source, unverified). Download format: MP3, WAV, or AIFF per one source — [StreamScheme](https://www.streamscheme.com/soundsnap-review/). **A monthly (no annual commitment) option does appear to exist** across all three sources, so a one-month plan is possible. Given the price conflicts, confirm exact current pricing at soundsnap.com/pricing directly before purchase.
- **Zapsplat**: free tier is fully functional (150,000+ items) with attribution + download-speed/limit friction. "Gold" upgrade is explicitly framed as "a small monthly or yearly donation" (pay-what-you-want model), with reported reference figures ranging from £4/mo–£30/yr to £4.99/mo–£39.99/yr depending on the source and date — [zapsplat.com/support-us-and-upgrade](https://www.zapsplat.com/support-us-and-upgrade/), [BityClips aggregator](https://bityclips.com/tool/zapsplat/pricing). ## Which service gives the most quality per dollar for about 60-100 sounds needed within 3 days

### Takeaway
For a 3-day deadline needing 60-100 curated, game-legal, cartoon/racing-appropriate sounds, **Zapsplat Gold** is the strongest overall pick: its category tree already matches the exact asset list (cartoon impacts, whooshes, UI chimes, motor racing, casino/arcade), games/apps are explicitly permitted in the primary license text, WAV quality is available to paid members, sounds downloaded while subscribed stay licensed for life even after cancelling (so a single one-time "donation month" plausibly covers the whole project permanently), and the reported cost is the lowest of any service reviewed. **Soundsnap** is the strongest backup/supplement given its dedicated racing/tire-squeal tagging, if Zapsplat's arcade/cartoon categories come up short on kart-specific engine notes. **Splice Sounds+** ($9.99/mo, 100 credits) is a reasonable fallback if either of the above proves thin on a specific sound category, since its credits comfortably cover the full 60-100 count in one billing cycle.

### Cited Findings
- Zapsplat: games/apps explicitly permitted ("4. Games, apps, and software" in the enumerated permitted-uses list), WAV+MP3 for Premium/Gold, lifetime retention of the license for anything downloaded while Gold, and a category tree that maps almost one-to-one onto this game's sound list (Cartoon, Vehicles>Car, Sport>Motor Racing, Sound Design>Whoosh, Multimedia and UI, Leisure>Casino and Games Arcade) — [Zapsplat Standard License](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-type/standard-license/).
- Soundsnap: 500,000+ SFX with racing/tire-squeal-specific tagging already confirmed at the tag-page level, commercial/game use explicitly permitted, no-attribution license — [Soundsnap License](https://www.soundsnap.com/license), [tire_squeal tag](https://www.soundsnap.com/tags/tire_squeal).
- Splice Sounds+: $9.99/mo for 100 credits (1 sample = 1 credit), games explicitly listed as a permitted creative work, samples "yours to keep" after cancelling — [Melodyflare pricing](https://melodyflare.com/splice-sounds-price/), [WebSearch summary of Splice Sounds Licensing FAQ](https://support.splice.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025013734-Splice-Sounds-Licensing-FAQ).
- By contrast, Artlist (Enterprise-only for games) and Adobe Stock (Enterprise-only Extended audio license) both fail the "within 3 days" test outright, since Enterprise sales cycles for either would not plausibly close in 3 days — [Artlist Enterprise plan](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/33980035053213-Enterprise-plan-explained), [Adobe Stock FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/help/usage-licensing.html).

### Inferences
- "Quality per dollar" for this specific brief should be read as "quality per dollar per hour," since the deadline is as tight a constraint as the price — this favors the two services (Zapsplat, Soundsnap) whose taxonomy most closely pre-sorts the exact categories needed, minimizing search/audition time, over Freesound's technically-free but unsorted catalogue.
